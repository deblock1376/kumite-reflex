Why it works. Gaps between cues are random, so you can't time the rhythm and have to react to what you see. The ladder adds load one layer at a time: one stimulus with one answer, then several choices, then a rule you have to apply before moving, then reading a body cue the way you would read an opponent. Go/No-Go cues (Yame, feints) train you to hold still, which keeps you from getting drawn onto a counter. Camera timing uses the front camera to spot the instant you start moving after each cue and logs that as your reaction time. It all runs on the device. Tap to time is the fallback when a coach is holding the device, but the coach's own reaction adds about 150–200 ms. With either method, compare one session against another rather than against an absolute number.
